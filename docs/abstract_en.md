# Alpha221: Genetic Algorithm for Risk-Constrained Portfolio Optimization

Alpha221 is a command-line course prototype for comparing a genetic algorithm (GA), Markowitz optimization, and an equal-weight 1/N portfolio under an annualized volatility limit. Its asset universe is 15 stocks and two ETFs: SPY for the S&P 500 and ETFBW20TR.WA for WIG20TR. The GA portfolio allocates 80% to exactly 10 stocks (1–20% portfolio weight each) and 20% to the ETFs (at least 1% each). The objective is estimated probability of a positive compounded USD return over six months. Markowitz uses the same budgets, 20% stock cap, and ETF minimums without the exact-10 requirement; 1/N equally weights all stocks within the 80% bucket and both ETFs within the 20% bucket.

Market data are downloaded from Yahoo Finance through the unofficial yfinance library. Adjusted daily prices are converted to USD with historical USD/PLN and EUR/USD rates. The downloader keeps complete common trading dates without filling missing stock or ETF prices. It also creates weekly snapshots from the last common session of each Friday-ending week.

The validated snapshot from 2026-11-20 contains 485 common daily prices and 105 weekly prices, resulting in 104 weekly return intervals. Weekly statistics are annualized with 52 periods per year; daily statistics use 252. The downloaded price files are local and ignored by Git.

Data acquisition, conversion, validation, weekly block-bootstrap probability estimation, portfolio constraints, a five-year walk-forward test, and exhaustive evaluation of all 3,003 stock subsets are implemented. The estimator uses four-week blocks and 1,000 paths during GA optimization; reported probabilities use 10,000 paths and include two- and eight-week block sensitivity. Weights use a rolling two-year weekly lookback and are re-optimized every 26 weeks. Converting capital into asset units remains future work. Yahoo Finance data are used only for the closed educational project and are not redistributed.
