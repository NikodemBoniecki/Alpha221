# Alpha221: Genetic Algorithm for Risk-Constrained Portfolio Optimization

Alpha221 is a command-line course project that compares a genetic algorithm (GA), Markowitz optimization, and a 1/N portfolio under a volatility limit. The basket contains 15 stocks and two ETFs: SPY and ETFBW20TR.WA. GA assigns 80% to exactly 10 stocks, with 1-20% in each, and 20% to the ETFs. Its objective is the estimated chance of a positive six-month USD return. Markowitz uses the same budgets without the exact-10 rule, while 1/N splits both buckets equally.

The program gets market data from Yahoo Finance through the unofficial yfinance library. It converts adjusted daily prices to USD with historical USD/PLN and EUR/USD rates. Missing stock and ETF prices are not filled. Weekly prices come from the last common session of each Friday-ending week.

The checked dataset covers 2019-11-21 to 2026-11-20. It contains 1,703 common daily sessions and 366 weekly prices, giving 365 weekly return periods. The audit found no ticker or currency mismatches, invalid prices, or weekly gaps. Every required FX rate came from the same date. Weekly statistics use 52 periods per year and daily statistics use 252. Price and audit files stay local and are ignored by Git.

The program includes data checks, currency conversion, portfolio limits, weekly block bootstrap, a five-year walk-forward test, and a check of all 3,003 stock subsets. GA uses four-week blocks and 1,000 bootstrap paths during optimization. The report uses 10,000 paths and repeats the estimate for two- and eight-week blocks. Weights are fitted on a rolling two-year window and recalculated every 26 weeks. With a 22% risk limit, historical compounded returns were 199.71% for GA, 319.40% for Markowitz, and 177.29% for 1/N. All three methods had 9 positive six-month periods out of 10. The results exclude transaction costs, taxes, and rebalancing costs, so they do not prove that one method predicts better than another. Converting capital into asset units is still to be done.
